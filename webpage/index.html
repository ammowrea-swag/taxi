Taxi Cab Scams Growing at Airports

Police cited over 2,000 drivers for providing paid rides without a taxi or ride-for-hire license at JFK and LaGuardia airports in 2025, making up over half of all citations for the calendar year. Since 2019, citations for illegal rides have grown 35 percent.

By Ashley Mowreader
Published May 7, 2026

Millions of passengers fly through John F. Kennedy International and LaGuardia Airports each year. Thousands may have gotten home illegally.

An analysis of New York City data found over 2,000 drivers received citations for providing unlicensed rides-for-hire at JFK and LaGuardia, with over 1,732 citations filed at JFK alone. The data points to the city’s growing investment to impede illegal cab operations through increased security measures and policing strategies.

“We will not look the other way while unlicensed operators undermine our laws, take away revenue from hard-working, law-abiding TLC-licensed drivers, and put passengers at risk,” said James Parziale, spokesperson for the NYC Taxi and Limousine Commission.

But taxi advocates and drivers say existing efforts show cracks, casting doubts over if officials’ new measures can protect cabs and their passengers.

Risky Business

Taxi scams can be detrimental to various NYC stakeholders. For licensed drivers, unregulated cabs hurt their business and avoid paying city costs including licensing, insurance, inspections and taxes.

Becoming a licensed taxi or Uber requires significant investment of time and money. All rides-for-hire must be licensed with the Taxi and Limousine Commission and applicants must hold a Social Security number, pass a drug test and complete a three-day course. Receiving a proper taxi certification can cost over $875 dollars , not counting insurance coverage, which is an additional requirement.

For the unknowing passenger, unlicensed cabs are a safety risk, lacking background checks, proper identification and perhaps insurance, in addition to a monetary risk. Scam victims have reported paying as much as $800 for a ride from JFK to Manhattan, more than 10 times the $70 flat fare riders should pay in a yellow cab.

In recent years, NYC officials have created stricter measures to enforce unlicensed vehicle-for-hire operations. Under Local Law 32, any vehicle operator who operates outside of their proper license can face fines from $1,000 to $2,000 or imprisonment for 60 days, or both. This operation is led by the Taxi and Limousine Commission’s Uniformed Services Bureau in partnership with the Port Authority.

In December, the New Jersey Port Authority announced it will allocate $100 million over the next 10 years to increase regulation and enforcement of unlicensed cab operations at airports, as well.

“We are fully committed to protecting travelers from predatory ride solicitation, and we are determined to intensify our enforcement efforts to drive these fraudsters out of our airports and out of business,” said Port Authority spokesperson Seth Stein.

Airport Scams at JFK

A hotspot for taxi scams remains NYC’s airports, with John F. Kennedy Airport in Jamaica, Queens leading the trend. TLC data from the past six years shows the highest rate of illegal taxi cab enforcement across the city happening at JFK.

Data shared by the Port Authority shows even greater rates of taxi cab fraud citations with over 2,602 summons issued for JFK drivers and 272 to LGA drivers for a total of 2,874 summonses in 2025.

JFK is New York’s busiest airport, with over 1,400 planes from domestic and international destinations approved to fly in or out of the airport in summer 2025 compared to LaGuardia’s 1,140, receiving almost twice as many passengers daily.

“It’s a bigger airport, which means more customers and more areas where shenanigans can take place,” said Matthew Daus, a lawyer and researcher at the City College's University Transportation Research Center.

Construction at the airport has created additional confusion about approved pickup and drop-off zones, making new opportunities for scammers to take advantage of unknowing customers.

The airport has clearly designated taxi stalls where cabbies line up to pick up passengers as well as Uber and Lyft pickup zones, but after stepping outside, travelers are bombarded by individuals trying to orchestrate rides.

The growth in citations is due in part to increase enforcement, as well as increasing incidents, sources familiar with the matter said.

At JFK, “security personnel describe the operation as routine: the same faces appear day after day, circling the arrival areas, scanning for passengers to approach. When police show up, the drivers scatter, only to regroup minutes later once the coast is clear,” Daus wrote in a University Transportation Research Center report. “It is a cat-and-mouse game in which enforcement rarely seems to gain the upper hand.”

The Port Authority employs a range of strategies to reduce taxi scams, including utilizing uniformed and plainclothes officers to identify suspicious behaviors, restricting parking at terminal entrances and impounding unattended vehicles. In addition, the TLC has its own enforcement unit that partners with Port Authority officers to conduct operations.

LaGuardia, on the other hand, has seen a decline in taxi cab scams, which could be tied to the airport’s reconstruction and re-zoning of ride-share and taxi pickup locations, Daus said. Daus worked as a consultant for the project.

New Enforcement Methods

“Operation Legal Ride,” is the Port Authority’s $100 million campaign, designed to root out scammer cabs who try to pick up passengers at airports.

As part of the initiative, the Port Authority will purchase new tech, including AI-powered license-plate readers and closed-circuit camera systems as well as the establishment of an expanded database of unlicensed drivers. All of the efforts will be funded by legal taxi and ride share fees collected from passengers at the airport, according to a press release . The Port Authority has yet to announce any contract partners developing the tech for Operation Legal Ride.

Daus and others in the taxi industry argue that the Port Authority has been collecting fees for years that could have been allocated toward a scam-deterrant project like Operation Legal Ride.

Still, the efforts are “better late than never,” Daus said.

What's Not Working

<

But enforcement methods have also left some motorists with, what they claim to be, undue citations.

A 2023 lawsuit filed by Mobilization for Justice, Gupta Wessler PLLC and Pollock Cohen LLP filed on behalf of New Yorkers who had received citations from the Taxi and Limousine Commission at airports claims the TLC engages in predatory behaviors to collect money from unsuspecting drivers.

The suit alleges that, based on TLC data from 2019 to 2022, 86 percent of individuals receiving LL32 citations through sting operations are people of color, despite an estimated 56 percent of motorists dropping off passengers at JFK and LaGuardia being people of color.

New tech may help reduce the need for the TLC’s on-ground enforcement officers, and in turn reduce racial bias, but it could also further perpetuate biases against who is cited and who’s not.
